# 01 — Market: who pays for 3xDezine, and why

*Research date: 2026-10-16. Scope: India residential interiors and home improvement, with brief global context. All sources were found by web search. Reddit could not be fetched directly (blocked), so its evidence is thin; see "What I could not verify".*

**Confidence key:** **H** = filed financials or reputable press reporting them. **M** = company statements, trade press or consistent secondary sources. **L** = "market research" press-release sites (IMARC, Ken Research, Mordor, Blueweave, PS Market Research and the like), vendor blogs, or single anecdotes. These L-grade figures conflict with each other by 3–7x and should not go in a pitch deck without a caveat.

---

## Executive summary

1. **The market is large, but nobody measures it the same way.** "Home interiors" in India is quoted at **US$12–37 bn** depending on who defines it (L). The most commonly repeated figure is **US$20–30 bn** (Mordor via The Ken, 2021; HomeLane cites "over $30 bn", 2026). Treat any single number as ±50%.
2. **It is overwhelmingly unorganised.** The three largest platforms (Livspace, HomeLane with DesignCafe, and Bonito) together booked **≈₹2,300 cr (~US$270 m) in FY25**. That is **~1% of even the low-end market estimates** (my derived estimate). Local carpenters, contractors and independent designers still serve most homeowners (Inc42, Jul 2026).
3. **The platforms are not great businesses.** Livspace made ₹1,460 cr revenue in FY25 but a ₹242 cr net loss, then laid off ~1,000 staff (12–25%) in Feb 2026. HomeLane grew 22% to ₹748 cr in FY25 with a ₹111 cr loss; its FY26 revenue was "roughly flat" per the CEO, and it missed its ₹1,000 cr target twice. Bonito's revenue *fell* 25% in FY25 and its valuation was cut 18%. **Conclusion:** the full-stack, own-the-execution model burns cash, which leaves room for tools that help the fragmented majority.
4. **Ticket sizes:** a full 2 BHK interior runs **~₹5–15 lakh** and a 3 BHK **~₹7–20 lakh** at mid-range. Premium work is 2–3x that (M/L, mostly vendor blogs). **Modular kitchen plus wardrobes is the bulk of the spend**, quoted at **45–70%** of the budget.
5. **Demand drivers are mixed right now.** Top-7-city housing sales fell **14% in 2025 to ~3.96 lakh units**, although value rose 6% to ₹6 lakh cr (Anarock, Dec 2025). Home-loan disbursement grew only ~5% in H1 FY26 (NHB). The growth story is premiumisation, Tier-2/3 expansion and shorter renovation cycles (10–12 years, down from 15), not volume.
6. **Pain points, ranked by evidence:**
   - (1) delays and incomplete work;
   - (2) cost escalation and "indicative" quotes that grow, plus hidden charges;
   - (3) advance-payment and refund disputes;
   - (4) trust and communication breakdown;
   - (5) measurement or design-not-fitting-site.

   Consumer commissions are actively ordering refunds against NoBroker (Jul 2026), Bonito and Regalo Kitchens. Livspace, HomeLane and DesignCafe all sit at **~1.5/5 on Trustpilot** (small, self-selected samples).
7. **Designers and studios run on WhatsApp, Excel and SketchUp/AutoCAD.** Indian SaaS attempts at this workflow (InteriorOS, Verified Studio, KM CRM) are tiny or unproven. Paid tools already in use give price anchors: Coohom Pro at **~₹2,300/month**, SketchUp at **~$20/month**, and KD Max at **~₹90,000 per 3-year licence** for modular-kitchen makers.
8. **Best first paying customer: small design-and-build studios and turnkey contractors (1–10 people) in Tier-1/Tier-2 cities.** These are the people who quote fixed prices on 2–3 BHK jobs and absorb overruns. 3xDezine's BOQ-with-wastage, proposals with cost deltas, version history and client-view permissions directly target their worst problem, the quote dispute. Willingness to pay is modest (₹1–3k per seat per month), but the sales cycle is days and they are reachable through Instagram, IIID chapters and dealer counters.
9. **Second wave: material brands and dealers** (plywood, laminate, hardware, paint), as sponsors or channel partners. They already spend heavily on carpenter loyalty programmes (Pidilite's FCC claimed more than 1 lakh contractors in 2016). They pay more and sell slowly. **Modular-kitchen manufacturers pay the most** (₹90k licences), but 3xDezine lacks the cabinet and modular features they need.
10. **Gaps to close before charging:** quotation/PDF export with GST lines, labour line items, modular-kitchen and wardrobe modules, and a client approval link. The Maharashtra PWD SoR anchor is credible for civil works but is *not* how interior studios price carpentry. Market-rate overrides per firm are needed.

**Working assumption:** the residential interiors and fit-out spend that 3xDezine could touch (kitchens, wardrobes, carpentry, false ceiling, paint, flooring, electrical/lighting) is **~US$20–30 bn a year**. This is a judgement call, not a verified figure.

### Organised vs unorganised

- No reliable recent organised-share figure exists for *interiors*.
  - [Redseer](https://redseer.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Disruption-in-Indian-furniture-retailing-_-31-August-2018-v1.pdf) put *furniture* at ~14% organised (2018).
  - [Datum](https://datum.beehiiv.com/p/residential-furniture-market-reach-2855-billion-356-billion-2028) puts residential furniture 85% unorganised (2023).
  - A marketing blog ([Orange Owl](https://orangeowl.marketing/unicorn-chronicles/livspace-success-story/)) claims interiors are "~15% organised". This is L-grade and unsourced.
- **Derived check:** Livspace (₹1,460 cr) + HomeLane consolidated (₹748 cr) + Bonito (₹115 cr) ≈ **₹2,320 cr ≈ US$270 m in FY25**. Against a US$20–30 bn market, that is **≈1%**. Adding mid-size regional brands (Decorpot, Design Qube, Asian Paints Beautiful Homes, Godrej Interio's interior arm and others) probably puts "branded turnkey" at **low single digits**. The rest is independent designers, contractor-carpenter crews and the modular-kitchen and dealer ecosystem.
- Inc42 (Jul 2026): independent architects, designers and contractors "dominate through personalised service and pricing flexibility"; local carpenters and contractors "still serve most homeowners".

### Growth drivers (and headwinds)

- **New housing.** [Anarock](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/housing-sales-volume-down-14-in-2025-in-7-cities-anarock) (26 Dec 2025):
  - top-7-city sales were **~3,95,625 units in 2025**, down 14%, while sales value rose 6% to over ₹6 lakh cr;
  - launches rose 2% to ~4.19 lakh;
  - unsold stock rose 4% to ~5.77 lakh ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/markets/unsold-homes-up-4-in-2025-across-top-7-cities-as-new-supply-outstrips-demand-anarock)).

  Under-construction sales turn into interior demand at possession, typically 2–4 years later, so the 2021–2024 sales boom is reaching handover now. **Derived:** ~4 lakh units × ₹5–10 lakh each is roughly **₹20–40k cr ($2.3–4.7 bn) a year** of interior demand from top-7 new sales alone (L, my estimate).
- **Premiumisation.** Volume is down but value is up. Livspace attributes FY25 growth to "premium and mass-premium". HomeLane says its AOV "nearly doubled" since the pandemic (Inc42, 2026).
- **Home loans.** NHB: individual housing-loan disbursement was **₹4.36 lakh cr in H1 FY26 vs ₹4.15 lakh cr** a year earlier (~5%). Outstanding loans reached ₹37.2 lakh cr in Sep 2025. Rural outstanding grew 10.7% ([LoansJagat summarising NHB](https://www.loansjagat.com/news/indias-home-loan-demand-rises-as-buyers-search-for-cheaper-emis), Feb 2026) (M).
- **Tier-2/3.** HomeLane is expanding through franchise-owned stores into Nashik, Rajkot, Jaipur, Rourkela, Gangtok and similar cities. It reports 95 studios in 47 cities, all franchise-owned. Small-city stores do **₹15–20 lakh/month** vs ₹1–1.5 cr for metro stores ([Franchise India](https://www.franchiseindia.com/index.php/insights/en/news/homelane-hits-profitability-milestone-ramps-up-franchise-expansion-across-india.58124), 2025–26) (M). Livspace: 150+ stores in 90+ cities ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/livspace-posts-rs-1460-cr-revenue-in-fy25-losses-shrink-42-10559863), Oct 2025).
- **Renovation cycle** shortened from ~15 to 10–12 years (Indian Retailer, Jul 2024) (L).
- **Headwinds:** IT layoffs, high prices, a falling sales volume (Anarock). Some sources also say 18% GST applies to interior works contracts and design fees, and that many quotes exclude it ([RealtyHunting](https://realtyhunting.com/home-interior-cost-in-india/), L).

### Global context (brief)

- Global interior design *software* is estimated at ~US$5 bn (2024) → ~$10 bn (2033) ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/interior-design-software-market), L).
- Best hard comparable: **Manycore Tech (Kujiale / Coohom)** listed in Hong Kong on 17 Apr 2026. 2025 revenue was **RMB 820 m (~US$113 m)**, 96.9% from subscriptions, with a 23.2% share of China's spatial-design software market ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manycore_Tech); [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1125056920)) (M–H). This shows that a browser 3D design-plus-catalogue SaaS can reach scale in a fragmented, carpenter-heavy Asian market. Its model is selling to designers and furniture/kitchen makers, not homeowners.
- **Houzz Pro** (US): ~$55–399/month per tier ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/199689/Houzz-Pro/pricing/), 2025–26). Its value is leads, CRM, proposals and 3D floor plans together. This is the bundle Indian studios lack.

**Take rate:** these platforms are *full-stack*. Revenue is the whole project value, so "take rate" is really gross margin, ~50% at Livspace. Under the older Livspace Design Partner model, freelance designers paid a commission or, in Singapore listings, kept ~50% of project profit ([NewsMinute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/atom/livspace-invest-rs-70-crore-global-designer-partnership-initiative-69931), dated).

**What it says about the market:**

1. **Execution is the cost centre.** Delays, site supervision and rework need people, and that is what keeps these firms loss-making a decade in. The Ken (2021) said the platforms still struggled with "delayed delivery, increased costs, lack of communication".
2. Growth has slowed (HomeLane flat in FY26; Bonito shrinking) while marketing is being cut. Customer acquisition is expensive.
3. Both leaders are now betting on **AI design automation** to cut designer hours. Design and quoting software is seen as the lever, which supports a tool thesis.
4. ~1% organised share after 10+ years and ~$1 bn+ of VC funding suggests homeowners will not move en masse to platforms. **The fragmented majority stays and needs better tools.** A SaaS selling to that majority does not compete with the platforms on execution risk.

One designer's description of the workflow: "Excel becomes the BOQ… WhatsApp becomes the place for vendors, contractors and quick client updates" | **Medium** (strong supply-side signal, little consumer data) |
| 7 | **Material substitution** (lower-grade ply or hardware than quoted) | Mentioned in reviews ("unsuitable materials", HomeLane Trustpilot) and in buyer guides advising brand and grade in writing | **Weak–medium** (widely believed, poorly documented) |
| 8 | **Scope changes** | Implicit in "add-ons and customisation change price" | **Weak** as a standalone item; overlaps with #2 |

**Mapping to 3xDezine:** the product's strongest fit is with pains #2, #6 and partly #5: a buffered BOQ, proposals with cost deltas, version history, View/Finishes/Layout edit permissions, and dimensioned plans with NBC checks. It does not help with #1 or #3 (execution and payments). Those need milestones and payments, which are out of scope today.

## 5. Best first paying customer

| Segment | Pain fit to current product | Willingness to pay | Reachability | Sales cycle | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Small design-build studios / turnkey contractors (1–10 ppl, Tier-1/2)** | **High.** They quote fixed prices, absorb wastage and overruns, and fight change-order disputes. BOQ with wastage and contingency, proposals and cost deltas, and client-view permissions replace Excel plus WhatsApp. 3D wins the client faster than SketchUp/V-Ray turnaround | Low–medium: ~₹1–3k/seat/month (anchors: Coohom ₹2,300/month; Excel BOQ templates at ₹49–199 show the floor). Higher if it demonstrably wins jobs | High: Instagram, YouTube, IIID chapters, dealer counters, WhatsApp groups | Days to weeks; self-serve | **First target** |
| **Independent design-only consultants** | Medium. They need renders and drawings more than BOQs; SketchUp/AutoCAD lock-in | Low | High | Short | Include, but they will churn without CAD export |
| **Homeowners directly** | Medium. Planning and budgeting help, but one-off use (buy once per 10 years) | Very low (free apps exist) | Expensive (paid ads) | n/a | Use as a **free viral loop**: homeowners invite their designer or contractor, or vice versa. Not the payer |
| **Material dealers / brands** | Medium. Product placement in the 24-material library, BOQ leading to orders, carpenter loyalty | **High budgets** (marketing, influencer programmes) | Low–medium (enterprise sales) | 3–9 months | **Second wave**: sponsored catalogues, a "pay per specified SKU" model, distribution to their carpenter and designer networks |
| **Modular kitchen / wardrobe makers** | Low today (needs cabinets, cut lists, CNC) | **Highest** (₹90k–1 L licences for KD Max/KCD) | Medium | Months | Later, once a modular module exists |
| **Builders / developers** | Low–medium (buyer customisation portals, standard plans) | Medium–high per deal | Low | Long; few logos | Opportunistic pilots only |
| **Turnkey platforms** | Low (they build in-house) | — | — | — | Competitors and benchmark, not customers |

**Reasoning:**

- **Who bears the cost risk** is who values accurate costing. In India that is the studio or contractor who quotes a fixed ₹6–12 L 2 BHK job and loses margin to wastage, pack rounding, forgotten items and argued change orders. Homeowners suffer too, but they buy interiors once a decade and won't subscribe.
- **The product's differentiator is credible numbers plus sharing.** Few Indian tools combine a 3D render with a live BOQ that updates as finishes change. Proposals with cost deltas map directly to the "indicative quote that grew" dispute. Edit permissions let a studio share with clients safely (client sees, comments or changes finishes only). This is a client-facing approval artefact that replaces WhatsApp screenshots.
- **Reachability and speed:** small studios buy self-serve on a card or UPI after a demo video. A ₹999–2,499/month plan with a free tier for homeowners is a realistic anchor. Validate by talking to 15–20 studios in Pune or Mumbai first, given the Maharashtra SoR anchor and the city's high sales volume (MMR plus Pune = ~49% of top-7 sales in 2025).
- **Pricing caution:** PWD SoR rates are for public civil works. Studio carpentry and modular rates are market-driven and city-specific. Let firms override rates and save their own rate cards, or the BOQ will look "wrong" to the very people paying for it.

**Must-haves before they pay** (based on what competitors bundle and the pain evidence):

- branded PDF quotation/BOQ with GST and payment-milestone lines;
- labour and installation line items;
- a basic modular kitchen and wardrobe block library (60–70% of ticket value);
- a client approval link that records accept/reject with timestamps;
- DWG/PDF export for the carpenter.
