# 02 — Competitive landscape and pricing (India)

*Research date: 2026-10-16. All prices were read on that date unless a source date says otherwise. Converted INR figures use about ₹88 = US$1; that rate is an assumption, so check it before quoting. Vendor pages that could not be read directly are marked **UNVERIFIED** and listed in section 9.*

---

## 1. Summary

- **The market has three groups, and no tool covers all of them for an Indian designer at an Indian price.**
  1. **Visualisers**: Foyr Neo, Coohom, Homestyler, Planner 5D, RoomSketcher, HomeByMe, and the AI renderers (Interior AI, Spacely, RoomGPT). They make good-looking pictures. Their quantities are shallow, they have no defensible BOQ, and they have no GST or Indian rate data.
  2. **Manufacturing CAD for modular furniture**: imos, Cabinet Vision, 2020 Design, Polyboard/OptiCut, and Infurnia, which is Indian. They produce panel-accurate cut lists and CNC output, and quote from panels and hardware. They cost a lot (Infurnia ₹34,000/month for 4 seats; Polyboard about US$2.5–4.8k one-time; imos and Cabinet Vision by quote only) and they are built for factories, not for a designer quoting a 2BHK.
  3. **Business tools for running a studio**: Houzz Pro/Ivy, DesignFiles, Mydoma, Programa, and Studio Designer. They cover proposals, invoices, client portals and payments. They are priced in USD at US$55–199 per user per month (about ₹4.8k–17.5k), are built around US sales tax and QuickBooks, have no Indian catalogue, and do not link to a 3D model. In India this group is mostly **Excel/Google Sheets plus WhatsApp**, plus some very cheap or free Indian toolkits: StudioMatrx Pro at ₹999/year, myBillBook templates, and Construction-Estimator-India Excel BOQs.
- **The big Indian turnkey firms built their own tools.** Livspace has Canvas, a 3D tool with catalogue and BOQ tracking. HomeLane has SpaceCraft, a browser 3D walkthrough with a live budget. This shows the workflow (3D linked to a budget, co-browsing with the client) wins sales. Their own designers report closing orders in 7 days instead of 30–45, but that is HomeLane's claim. Independent designers and small studios cannot get these tools.
- **3xDezine already does one thing no affordable tool does:** a buffered material BOQ in INR tied to the 3D model, with a defensible rate source (the PWD SoR) and plan-rule checks. It is missing the three things a designer needs to *get paid*: labour, a GST quotation, and client approval on a scope. It also has no modular kitchen or wardrobe module.
- **Recommended segment:** independent interior designers and 1–5 person studios in Tier-1 and Tier-2 Indian cities who do residential turnkey work (2–3BHK, ₹5–25 lakh jobs) and today use SketchUp (often unlicensed), Excel and WhatsApp.
- **Recommended price:** a free tier, then **₹799/month (₹7,999/year) Solo** and **₹1,999/month (₹19,999/year) Studio (3 seats)**, plus a **₹499 one-project pass**. This sits above Homestyler and Planner 5D Premium, near Foyr Basic, and far below Infurnia, Houzz Pro and licensed SketchUp Pro plus a renderer. See section 7.

## 5. Gaps: what no affordable tool in India does well

1. **A defensible BOQ generated from the 3D model.** The visualisers give a shopping list. StudioMatrx and Excel give a BOQ from typed-in quantities. Only factory CAD (₹8.5k+ per seat per month) produces real quantities, and only for carcass panels. None covers civil and finishing work (tiling, flooring, false ceiling, paint, electrical points), estimates wastage, or rounds to pack sizes the way 3xDezine already does. No tool cites a public rate source (SoR) that a client or bank can check.
2. **A GST-correct quotation from the same model.** That means HSN/SAC codes, CGST/SGST vs IGST by place of supply, the split between works-contract (18%) and supply-only lines, design fee as a separate line, and payment milestones (for example 10/40/40/10). Today it is done by hand in Excel or myBillBook. The US tools assume sales tax and QuickBooks.
3. **Client approval on a versioned scope.** In India, changes in the middle of a project and disputes over "you said laminate, not acrylic" are a large share of the pain. Houzz, DesignFiles and Studio Designer have portals, but none ties an approved version of the 3D model to the BOQ with a signed delta. 3xDezine's proposal and review with cost deltas is 70% of this already.
4. **Indian brand catalogues with live or recent prices.** Century, Greenply and CenturyPly boards; Merino and Greenlam laminates; Hettich, Häfele, Ebco and Blum hardware; Kajaria, Somany and Johnson tiles; Asian Paints and Berger; Jaquar and Hindware. No affordable tool keeps these with dated MRP or dealer prices. Hettich and Häfele in India steer designers to showrooms rather than self-serve planners ([Hettich Plan](https://www.hettich.com/en-gr/services/hettich-plan), [Häfele India](https://home.hafeleindia.com/blogs/drawer-organisation/complete-guide-to-planning-a-premium-kitchen-design-with-hafele)).
5. **Modular kitchen and wardrobe design at designer scale.** Shutter, carcass and hardware configuration, an 8 ft / 7 ft wardrobe grid, loft units, L/U/parallel kitchens, and a price per running foot. Infurnia and imos do this for factories. Coohom does it but without an Indian catalogue. The solo designer's choices are SketchUp (often unlicensed) plus a component library.
6. **Plan compliance checks.** No interior or home tool checks NBC 2016 (minimum room sizes, ventilation, stair dimensions, egress). 3xDezine is the only one.
7. **WhatsApp-native workflow.** Indian clients approve on WhatsApp. No tool sends a link that opens a fast, phone-friendly 3D view plus a BOQ PDF with "Approve" and "Comment" buttons. StudioMatrx adds WhatsApp branding to share pages, and that is all.
8. **Low-end Android and patchy-network performance.** The cloud renderers need bandwidth. SketchUp, Enscape and D5 need GPUs. Many Tier-2 designers and most clients use ₹10–20k Android phones. A lightweight viewer (WebGL2 fallback, low-poly LOD, cached offline project) is an open niche.
9. **Labour plus material in one estimate.** Carpenter, mason, painter, electrician and plumber rates per unit (sqft, running ft, point) per city. Today this is done by hand everywhere except contractor tools like SiteSetu.

## 6. 3xDezine positioning

### 6.1 Where it already wins
- **The only affordable tool whose BOQ is calculated from the 3D model, with wastage, pack rounding, contingency and a cited rate source (the Maharashtra PWD SoR).** That makes a quote defensible to a client, and to a bank or CA for home-loan or top-up purposes.
- **NBC 2016 checks with suggested fixes.** No competitor has this. It matters for apartment renovations that move walls or wet areas, and for architects.
- **Proposals and review with cost deltas, version history, and View/Finishes/Layout/Full permissions.** That is the foundation for scope control, which Indian designers need most.
- **Indian plan templates** (2/3BHK typologies), an **Android app**, and **INR-first pricing**.
- **Real-time WebGPU 3D in the browser** with no install.

### 6.2 Where it is weak
- **No path to getting paid:** no labour, no GST quotation, no invoice, no milestones, no client approval or e-sign.
- **No modular kitchen or wardrobe module.** In Indian residential interiors, modular woodwork is typically the largest share of job value. This is the biggest product gap.
- **No render or video export.** Designers win jobs with images sent on WhatsApp. Foyr, Coohom and Homestyler set the expectation at 4K stills in under a minute.
- **No AI** ("restyle this room", photo → plan, prompt → finishes). The market now expects it.
- **Rates come from a single state's SoR.** Designers outside Maharashtra need city multipliers, and they price from dealer or brand rates more than PWD rates.
- **WebGPU reach:** older Android devices and browsers may not render. A fallback is needed.
- **Small library** compared with Coohom's and Homestyler's catalogues of 100k+ models.

### 6.3 Target segment and the 3–5 features that make 3xDezine clearly better
**Segment:** independent IDs and 1–5 person turnkey studios in Indian Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities doing ₹5–25 lakh residential jobs. Their stack today is SketchUp (often unlicensed) plus Excel plus WhatsApp. Their pain is turning a design into a quote quickly, getting it approved, and stopping scope creep.

1. **A GST quotation generated from the model.** Material plus labour lines (city labour rate cards per unit), design fee, HSN/SAC, CGST/SGST or IGST, payment milestones, and a branded PDF. *Nobody under ₹8k per month does this from a 3D model.*
2. **A WhatsApp approval link.** A lightweight phone viewer with a 3D walkthrough, the BOQ and the quote, and Approve / Request change buttons. Approval locks a version, and later proposals show the ₹ delta against the approved baseline. This extends the existing proposals, history and permissions.
3. **A modular kitchen and wardrobe configurator with an Indian hardware catalogue.** Parametric carcass, shutter finish (laminate, acrylic, PU, veneer), Hettich/Häfele/Ebco hardware sets, price per running ft and per sqft, and a simple cut list. It does not need to be CNC-grade. It needs to be quote-grade, priced from brand MRP.
4. **4K still export plus AI restyle.** Server-side or path-traced render of the current view, plus "apply style / suggest finishes within ₹X budget" that changes materials and updates the BOQ live. *AI that respects the budget* is a strong differentiator against Interior AI and Spacely, which are not dimensionally accurate.
5. **City and brand rate packs.** The SoR stays as the baseline. Add city multipliers and dated brand price lists with "last updated" stamps, so the BOQ stays defensible outside Maharashtra.

## 7. Price positioning

### 7.1 What Indian designers pay today, and what they pirate
- **Legitimate spend.** A licensed "pro" stack of SketchUp Pro (≈ ₹35k/yr + GST) plus Enscape (≈ ₹50k/yr + 18% IGST) comes to **about ₹1 lakh/yr per seat**. AutoCAD is about ₹1.05 lakh + GST per year ([IIFT Bangalore, May 2026](https://www.iiftbangalore.com/blog/best-software-for-interior-designers-in-india/)). An Indian design-education guide puts studio software budgets at ₹1.5–2 lakh per year per workstation ([StudioMatrx digital-skills guide](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/digital-skills)). Most small studios do not spend this.
- **Piracy evidence. No current survey is specific to designers, but the signals agree:**
  - India's unlicensed software rate was 58% in BSA's last published survey (2015 data, released 2016), down from 65% in 2009 ([BSA](https://www.bsa.org/news-events/news/use-of-unlicensed-software-at-58-percent-in-india-new-bsa-survey-finds)). No newer India figure was found.
  - The Delhi High Court said in *Autodesk v. Prashant Deshmukh* (2011) that pirated use "may be far exceeding" licensed use, and granted a permanent injunction and punitive damages ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/742253/)).
  - ArchitectureLive (2024-07-18) reports that Autodesk-appointed agencies made unannounced audit visits and threats to Indian architecture studios. It acknowledges piracy is prevalent and quotes critics who say subscriptions made the software unaffordable for small firms ([ArchitectureLive](https://architecture.live/harassment-threats-and-intimidation-many-architects-in-india-report-autodesks-aggressive-tactics/)).
  - IIFT Bangalore (May 2026) notes that pirated software is common among Indian design students.
  - Interior design firms in nearby markets have been raided over unlicensed V-Ray and Autodesk software (Malaysia 2022; Singapore, PDM International).
  - **Conclusion (inference):** most solo Indian IDs run unlicensed SketchUp, AutoCAD and V-Ray, so their *actual* cash spend on design software is about ₹0–10k per year. They do pay for Canva (₹4,999/yr), cheap Homestyler or Planner 5D plans, and per-render fees. The real competitor is "free and pirated plus Excel", so 3xDezine has to win on **time-to-quote and getting paid**, not on modelling.
- **Willingness to pay.** Indian designers do pay for tools that directly earn money: leads, quoting, and things that win jobs. Livspace and HomeLane designers accept large commissions in exchange for leads plus tooling. StudioMatrx sets a very low ₹999/yr anchor for spreadsheet-grade BOQ and quotes.

### 7.2 Recommended INR price ladder (GST extra)

| Tier | Price | Who | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | ₹0 | Students, homeowners, trial users | 1 active project, all templates, 3D, NBC checks, BOQ *view* (no export), watermark on shares, View-only links |
| **Project Pass** | **₹499 per project** (90 days) | Homeowners, occasional designers | One project unlocked: BOQ PDF/CSV, GST quote, WhatsApp approval link, 10 HD renders |
| **Solo** | **₹799/mo or ₹7,999/yr** | Independent IDs | Unlimited projects; BOQ + labour + **GST quotation**; branded PDF; WhatsApp approval links with version lock; proposals and history; 50 HD / 10 4K renders per month; city rate packs |
| **Studio** | **₹1,999/mo or ₹19,999/yr (3 seats)**, +₹499 per extra seat per month | 2–10 person studios | Everything in Solo, plus the role permissions (Finishes/Layout/Full), **modular kitchen and wardrobe configurator** with brand catalogues, team library, client portal with milestones and payment links, 200 HD / 40 4K renders, priority support |
| **Business / Brand** | from ₹9,999/mo (quote) | Turnkey firms, modular brands, dealers | Custom catalogue and rate cards, API, multi-branch, cut-list export, white-label viewer |
| Add-ons | ₹99 per 4K render pack (5); AI credits ₹199/100 | — | Pay-per-use, like Homestyler's ₹188 per 4K render |

**Rationale**
- ₹799 Solo sits **above** Homestyler Pro/Master (₹325–657) and Planner 5D Premium (₹425), because 3xDezine sells *quotes*, not pictures. It is **about 70% below** Foyr Basic (~₹2,900), SketchUp Pro (~₹2,900) and the US business tools (₹4.8–8.7k). One won ₹10 lakh job, at a typical 10–15% design fee, pays for about 10 years of Solo.
- Studio at ₹1,999 for 3 seats (≈ ₹666 per seat) is **about 92% below Infurnia's ₹8,500 per seat**. It is positioned as "Infurnia-lite" for studios that do not run CNC.
- The annual price gives about two months free; Indian SMB buyers prefer annual UPI or card payment once tax and invoicing are sorted. Support **UPI Autopay** for monthly plans.
- The ₹499 Project Pass meets pay-per-project demand (Coohom's "one-time pass", Homestyler pay-per-render) and works as a homeowner-to-designer acquisition channel.
- Offer **student pricing at ₹1,499/yr** to compete with free educational licences and to reduce piracy habits early (Foyr gives students up to 85% off).
- Avoid the Houzz-style traps that dominate its negative reviews: no forced annual contract after a trial, self-serve cancellation, and clear renewal emails.
